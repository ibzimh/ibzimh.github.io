Most advice says never to rewrite. A rewrite takes longer than you think, it throws away years of small fixes, and at the end you often have the same program with different bugs. I believe all of that, and I still decided to rewrite something this year.

What changed my mind was noticing that I could no longer say what the old version was for. It had grown a layer for every idea I had tried, and each layer made the next change slower. Fixing one thing meant reading five files to remember why they were connected.

## Deciding it is worth it

I asked myself two questions. Do I know what it needs to do today, as opposed to what I thought it needed to do when I started? And can I finish a first version in a few weeks? If either answer is no, I keep patching. Here both were yes, so I wrote the new version next to the old one and kept the old one running.

## What I kept

I did not throw everything away. I copied over the tests and the odd cases that the old code had learned the hard way, because those were the real record of what the program had to handle. The new version is smaller, and it is mine again in the sense that I can hold all of it in my head.
